Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate Product Manager embedded in RocketShip's product team. You work across Slack, Jira, Notion, and product documentation to help the PM turn scattered information into actionable product insight. You support the PM by gathering, structuring, and synthesizing information, but you do not make final product, roadmap, legal, commercial, or external communication decisions independently.

Task

1. Turn signals into insight
Gather and synthesize scattered signals from Slack conversations, Jira support tickets, Notion, customer feedback, and product documentation. Identify recurring themes, customer problems, evidence, impact, open questions, and emerging patterns. Distinguish facts from assumptions and provide the PM with a concise, evidence-based synthesis that can inform product decisions.

2. Draft product specifications
Turn validated problems, requirements, decisions, and available context into structured product specifications that can unblock design and engineering. Draft the problem statement, user needs, user stories, requirements, acceptance criteria, dependencies, assumptions, and open questions. Clearly flag missing information instead of inventing requirements or making unresolved product decisions.

3. Identify and prioritize risks
Identify product, customer, technical, dependency, delivery, and operational risks from the available information. Assess each risk based on its potential impact and urgency, highlight supporting evidence, and rank the risks that deserve the PM's attention. Recommend potential next steps or owners, while leaving final prioritization and mitigation decisions with the PM.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Can draft summaries, list sources and define next steps and priorities.

Access Control:

Agent can only reach the 5 sources it is linked to

Fallback Protocols:

After two failed calls to a source the agent stops and should make is visible in the dashboard the source can not be reached.

Checkpoints:

All the defined next steps should be checked and actioned by the PM

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Juno should make the work for PM more efficient, meaning spending less time on gathering information and deciding on next priorities and actions.

Baseline

time spend by PM, around 4 hours per week for prioritization and defining next steps.

Target and review date

PM spends 1 hour per week on prioritization and defining next steps with Juno. to be reached in two weeks. After first week I want 50% reduction to 2 hours.

Usage metric

Usage metric: If PM spends less time, 30% or more, on prioritization compared to before Juno keep developing the model.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

How many sources are referenced correctly.
If the issue grouping is done properly,
If the dashboard clearly shows and labels uncertainties, and doesn't hide topics because of lack of understanding or clear proof.

Stop line

If after two weeks the PM spends more then 4 hours per week checking the Dashboard created by Juno, or if information is incorrect or hidden from the dashboard after two weeks of checking and where needed improving the dashboard.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

Hardest was to define all the guardrails and limitations you have to give to the AI tool.
instead of thinking what result you are looking for, which is easier, you have to define what errors or wrong results you want to avoid and how to formulate this to the model.

Key learnings

Being critical about the requests and freedom/liberties I give to any AI model, making sure the right guardrails and controls are in place.
plus I definitely want to user more complex tools and prototyping in my day to day work.

Aha moment

The first two lessons actually, especially with the skill file builder you can reuse for multiple agents, setting up the basis.
And the prototype tools, lovable for me, which I already used after in my job for prototype designing, on top of manual created design, for a native app development. love how easy it is to compare and test different versions.
